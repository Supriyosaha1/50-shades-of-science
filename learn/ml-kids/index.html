How Do We Learn?

How do you know an apple is an apple when you see one?

Can you see how your brain works? You did not memorize a 50-page rulebook. When you see a fruit, your brain compares its clues (color, shape, peel) to memories of examples you have seen before!

What to Notice

Can you see what happens when you click on the Green Apple 🍏? Look closely! Even though the color is green, your brain did NOT get tricked into calling it an orange. Now change it—click the Orange 🍊 above! Notice how your brain instantly switches memories because of the bumpy peel and citrus shape.

🧠

How It Actually Works

Why does your brain do this? Your brain stores previous examples (a personal memory dataset). When a new fruit appears, your eyes measure multiple clues together: color, size, shape, and skin texture. No two apples are ever 100% identical, so your brain learns the flexible pattern rather than memorizing a single rigid picture!

Core Insight

We learn to recognize things by looking at examples.
